Guide for expats in Hong Kong

UK mortgages for Hong Kong residents, including first-time buyers

Yes. People living in Hong Kong can get a UK mortgage to buy a home, buy for family, invest in buy-to-let or remortgage, and first-time buyers can be considered too. You will usually need a specialist lender rather than a high street bank, a deposit of at least 25%, and clear evidence of your income in Hong Kong dollars. Here is how lenders look at Hong Kong residents and what you need to have ready.

The short answer

Living in Hong Kong does not stop you getting a UK mortgage. Hong Kong residents buy UK property every year: homes for a future move, flats for children at university, and buy-to-let investments. What changes is who will lend to you and on what terms.

Most UK high street banks either decline applicants who live overseas or only help existing customers. The market for Hong Kong residents is made up of specialist expat lenders, the international arms of UK banks, some building societies and private banks. They accept income in Hong Kong dollars, but they usually ask for a larger deposit than a UK resident would need, and they look closely at your job, your nationality, your pay package and your UK credit history.

Who can apply from Hong Kong

  • British expats living in Hong Kong have the widest choice. A UK connection, such as a previous UK address, a UK bank account or a UK credit history, helps.
  • Hong Kong permanent residents and other nationalities can also get UK mortgages, particularly for buy-to-let. Fewer lenders are available, and deposits and minimum incomes can be higher. Some lenders only accept certain nationalities.
  • Holders of a British National (Overseas) passport. Lenders do not all treat a BN(O) passport in the same way as British citizenship, so the choice of lender depends on your full situation.
  • If you are planning to move to the UK, some lenders will lend before you relocate and others prefer you to be living in the UK first, when different lenders and criteria apply. It is worth planning the timing before you choose a property.

Your Hong Kong dollar income

A UK mortgage is lent and repaid in pounds, so the lender converts your Hong Kong dollar income into sterling and often reduces it, commonly by 10% to 25%, to allow for exchange rate movements. The Hong Kong dollar is linked to the US dollar within a band of 7.75 to 7.85 Hong Kong dollars to the US dollar, which makes the income more predictable, and some lenders treat it more favourably as a result. Your income in pounds still moves with the pound against the US dollar.

Lenders work from your gross income. Many Hong Kong residents work in banking and financial services, where pay often includes:

  • Bonuses, usually averaged over one or two years, and some lenders count only half or another proportion.
  • Deferred awards and shares, which some lenders count once they have vested and others ignore.
  • Housing allowances, counted in full, in part or not at all depending on the lender.

Where income is paid in a foreign currency, changes in exchange rates may affect the amount you need to repay in sterling. For a mortgage on a home you or your family will live in, the lender must also have arrangements to limit that risk and must warn you if movements change what you owe or your payments by more than 20%.

First-time buyers living in Hong Kong

Being a first-time buyer and living abroad at the same time narrows the choice of lender, but it does not rule you out. Some lenders do not accept first-time buyers who live overseas, while others do, often with a larger deposit or a higher minimum income. A strong, steady income and a clear source of deposit matter more than usual.

Two points catch people out:

  • Owning a flat in Hong Kong means you are not a first-time buyer, for lenders or for stamp duty. Owning property anywhere in the world counts.
  • Buying for a child at university is common, but how you buy matters. Lending for a home a family member will live in, sometimes called a family let, is offered by fewer lenders than a standard buy-to-let, and putting the property in your child's name, or in both your names, changes which lenders and stamp duty rates apply.

Deposit and how much you can borrow

Most lenders for Hong Kong residents lend up to 75% of the property value, so plan for a deposit of at least 25%. Some ask for 30% or more for foreign nationals, first-time buyers, new build flats or high value properties.

For a home you will live in, many lenders use around 4.5 times your income as a guide, after they have converted it into pounds and applied any currency reduction. They also look at your commitments, including any Hong Kong mortgage or rent, so the actual amount offered can be lower. Keep a clear record of where your deposit came from, and allow for the exchange rate and transfer costs when you move it to the UK.

Buy-to-let and limited companies

For a buy-to-let mortgage, the loan is based mainly on the rent, which is paid in pounds. Lenders test that the rent covers the interest at a stress rate, usually by 145% for individuals and 125% for limited companies, so buy-to-let has more lenders available to Hong Kong residents than mortgages for your own home. Many lenders still set a minimum personal income.

Some investors buy through a UK limited company, which has different lenders, costs and tax treatment. Rental income from a UK property is usually taxable in the UK even though you live in Hong Kong, and landlords overseas normally register under the Non-Resident Landlord Scheme. Tax depends on your circumstances, so speak to a tax adviser.

If you already own a UK property, you can remortgage it to a new rate or release equity from Hong Kong. Your existing lender may offer you only limited products while you live overseas, so compare the wider market well before your current deal ends.

Stamp duty for Hong Kong residents

In England and Northern Ireland, buyers who are not UK resident pay a 2% surcharge on top of the standard Stamp Duty Land Tax rates. You are usually treated as non-UK resident if you were in the UK for fewer than 183 days in the 12 months before you buy. If you then spend enough time in the UK soon after the purchase, you may be able to claim the surcharge back.

Anyone who already owns a residential property anywhere in the world, including Hong Kong, and is not replacing their main home, also pays a further 5%.

First-time buyer relief can still apply to non-UK residents, with the 2% surcharge added on top. It is only available if every buyer has never owned a home anywhere in the world, you intend to live in the property as your only or main home, and the price is £500,000 or less. The documents you will need

Lenders and solicitors must check who you are, where you live and where your money comes from. A typical list for a Hong Kong resident is:

  • Passport and Hong Kong identity card
  • Proof of your Hong Kong address, such as a recent utility bill or bank statement
  • Employment contract, three to six months of payslips and bank statements showing your salary paid in
  • Your latest salaries tax Notice of Assessment, and letters confirming any bonus or deferred awards
  • For business owners, your Business Registration Certificate, at least two years of accounts and profits tax returns
  • Proof of your deposit, showing how it was built up

Documents not in English may need a certified translation. Most of the process can be handled remotely, with electronic signing and video calls. Hong Kong is eight hours ahead of the UK in winter and seven hours ahead in summer.

Before you apply

  • Keep a UK footprint. A UK bank account and a UK credit card used and cleared each month help a lender see your UK history.
  • Check your basic salary on its own. If the numbers only work with every bonus and award included, the choice of lender narrows quickly.
  • Decide whose name the property goes in before you make an offer, as it affects lenders and stamp duty.
  • Leave room for the exchange rate. Borrowing at the very top of what you could afford today leaves little margin if the pound strengthens.
  • Avoid applying to several lenders yourself. Each application leaves a mark on your credit file. A Decision in Principle from a lender that accepts Hong Kong residents tells you where you stand first.

Questions from Hong Kong residents

Can I get a UK mortgage if I live in Hong Kong?

Yes. Specialist UK lenders accept Hong Kong residents, both British expats and other nationalities, for buying a home, buy-to-let and remortgages. Most high street banks do not, so the choice of lender is narrower than for someone living in the UK.

Can I get a UK mortgage as a first-time buyer living in Hong Kong?

Yes, with some lenders. Fewer lenders accept first-time buyers who live overseas, and those that do often ask for a larger deposit or a higher minimum income. Owning a property in Hong Kong means you are not a first-time buyer.

How much deposit do I need if I live in Hong Kong?

Usually at least 25% of the purchase price, as most lenders for Hong Kong residents lend up to 75% of the value. Some ask for 30% or more for foreign nationals, first-time buyers or certain property types.

Will UK lenders accept my salary in Hong Kong dollars?

Many specialist lenders accept HKD income. They convert your gross income into pounds and often reduce it to allow for exchange rate movements. Bonuses, deferred awards and housing allowances are treated differently by each lender.

Can I get first-time buyer relief on stamp duty if I live in Hong Kong?

Possibly. Non-UK residents can claim first-time buyer relief if every buyer has never owned a home anywhere in the world, they intend to live in the property as their main home and the price is £500,000 or less, but the 2% non-resident surcharge is added on top. Your solicitor confirms the final figure.

Can I buy a UK property for my child at university?

Yes, but the way you buy matters. Fewer lenders offer mortgages on a property a family member will live in than on a standard buy-to-let, and whose name the property is in affects which lenders and stamp duty rates apply.
